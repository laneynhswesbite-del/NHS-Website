For Future Members

Before Membership

Practical, low-pressure suggestions for building toward NHS eligibility, starting as early as 9th grade.

9th Grade 10th Grade 11th Grade

NHS looks at four things: scholarship, service, leadership, and character. None of it happens overnight. These guides break down what that can look like at each stage, starting well before you're actually eligible to apply.

9th Grade

Building Your Foundation

You don't need to have it all figured out yet. Focus on starting good habits and finding what you actually enjoy.

Scholarship

NHS requires a 3.6 weighted GPA — aim higher now for a cushion later.

  • Aim for a 3.7+ weighted GPA this year
  • Use Flex for tutoring or a quiet study hall when you need it
  • Check Infinite Campus regularly and turn in assignments on time
  • Show up, be on time, and talk to teachers early if a class gets hard
  • Keep a planner so deadlines don't sneak up on you

Service

  • Join a club or two, ideally with friends. Watch for the club fair early in the year.
  • Clubs can be for fun or a career interest — both count, and sports/arts count too if outside of class
  • Try the local food bank, or volunteer through church or another community group
  • You don't need to track hours seriously yet, but jotting down what you do informally is a good habit to start

9th grade is about figuring out what you like. It's fine to drop something and try another. More isn't better — a few activities you stick with beat a long list you don't.

Leadership & Character

  • Leadership starts small: help out in class, offer to organize something for a club, take initiative when something needs doing
  • Just showing up and being reliable is a form of leadership
  • Stay out of trouble: get to class and Flex on time, follow school rules
  • Be someone teachers and peers can count on — a clean record now makes everything easier later
10th Grade

Narrowing Your Focus

By now you've had a year to try things out. Sophomore year is about narrowing down to what actually matters to you and getting more serious about it.

Fewer Activities, More Depth
  • Look back at what you tried freshman year and let go of what you didn't enjoy or weren't that involved in
  • Narrow down to 2–4 activities you actually want to commit to, instead of spreading thin across many
  • Depth and consistency matter more than a long list
Get More Actively Involved
  • Move from just attending to actually participating: volunteer for tasks, show up to events, take on a small role
  • Ask club officers or your adviser how you can help more
  • The more involved you are now, the more it'll show by junior year
Start Tracking Your Hours
  • Start a simple Google Doc or spreadsheet to log what you do: date, activity, hours, and a short description
  • Include service, leadership roles, and any notable school involvement
  • This isn't official NHS record-keeping yet, but the habit makes junior year, when hours start counting, much easier
11th Grade

Leading With Purpose

This is the year eligibility becomes real. If you meet the GPA requirement, you'll receive an emailed invitation to apply in mid-to-late February — see the Membership page for the full process. Junior year is about showing leadership, initiative, and real depth in the activities you've already invested in.

Step Into Leadership
  • Look for officer positions, captain roles, or other leadership opportunities in your chosen activities
  • Leadership doesn't require a title: organizing an event, mentoring a younger member, or leading a project all count
  • Take initiative — propose an idea or start something new instead of waiting to be asked
Show Depth and Impact
  • Focus on the few activities you've stuck with since 9th and 10th grade rather than adding new ones
  • Be able to talk about what you've actually contributed, not just attended
  • Genuine involvement in a few things matters more than a long resume
Employment Counts Too
  • A part-time job shows responsibility, work ethic, and time management — all part of leadership and character
  • Keep track of where you work and how long, just like your other activities
